## Anthropic Claude family

Claude is the strongest family for coding judgment and knowledge work. Its weaknesses are token burn, slowness to the first token, and Fable's weaker defence against prompt injection. All four models have a 1M context, 128K max output and effort levels low to max. Claude Code defaults to medium effort.

**Plans and pools:** All Claude models draw on one weekly allowance; Fable has its own cap inside it, not extra capacity. Subagents inherit the main session's model unless their definition sets one.

## OpenAI Codex family

OpenAI's line-up is the best value per task. Astra is a top-tier reviewer and long-run engine, Sol is the cheap strong daily driver, Terra does screens and prose, Luna does volume. The weak spots are frontend taste and over-literal prompts on the cheaper tiers. Spark retired on 14 Sep, and gpt-6-sol still runs but gpt-6.1-sol is the current recommendation.

**Plans and pools:** Codex and ChatGPT Work share one usage allowance, so switching to a cheaper model does not refill it. OpenAI publishes Plus estimates per 5 hours: Astra 5-45, Sol 15-160, Terra 35-285, Luna 350-3,000 local messages. Pin models explicitly.

## Google Gemini family (via Antigravity)

Gemini is the long-context and multimodal family, with strong prompt-injection resistance. The weak spots are terminal and agentic coding, slow and verbose output, and unreliable factual claims about code. The surrounding tooling (Antigravity quotas, headless mode, sandbox) is a bigger risk than the models themselves.

**Plans and pools:** In Antigravity, Gemini models and Claude/GPT models draw on separate usage meters, each with a weekly and a five-hour limit. Carbon needs Ultra or Enterprise, Argon needs a paid Pro plan or better, Hydrogen and 3.8 Flash are on every plan. Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 are on Pro for non-trial subscribers.

## Family personalities and cross-family review

Each family fails in its own way, which is why reviews across families catch more. The evidence that they do is thin but consistent.

| Family | Tends to | Watch for | Brief it with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claude | Be thorough, self-check, clean up and delete code, write well | Running for hours, spawning many subagents, high token burn, burying the point | A time or token budget and an explicit stop condition |
| OpenAI | Lead with the answer, follow the spec literally, stay compact (Sol); push hard on long runs (Astra) | Doing the bare minimum (Sol); sprawling diffs and runaway runs (Astra); ignoring AGENTS.md under pressure | A complete spec, acceptance tests, and a minimal-diff instruction for Astra |
| Gemini | Be ambitious, expand scope, read everything | Fabricated specifics, verbose output, judgment lagging coding; forum reports describe the agent leaving its project, so sandbox it | For reading and research, narrow questions, with every claim about code checked by another family; builds go through the Gemini build script and get reviewed like any other build |

## How to read these numbers

- Trust the independent runs (Artificial Analysis, Vals, Scale, LMArena) over launch tables. Within the top cluster, a gap of 2-3 points is noise.
- Vendor runs are flattering. Anthropic's own Terminal-Bench 4.0 run gave Sonnet 5.5 70.6% and Opus 5.5 66.4%. Independent harnesses give 63.6-64.1% and 59.6-65.2%. Google's claim that Argon trails Opus by 9 points shrinks to about 3 on AA's numbers.
- Effort settings change everything. Opus 5.5 scores 35 on the AA index at low effort and 59 at max, for $0.98 vs $10.04 a task. Vendor tables often compare one model at xhigh with another at medium.
- Index versions do not mix. AA moved to v5 on 27 Oct. Argon went from 53 to 50 and Gemini 3.8 Flash from 59 to 31 on the rebase. Discard any score dated before then.
- Fable's scores include its fallback. AA ran Fable 5.2 with safeguards on, so cyber and bio prompts were answered by Opus 4.8 or Opus 5. Its security numbers describe the routed system, not the model alone.
- Some boards are saturated or stale. SWE-bench Pro V2 Hard tops out near 98% with mixed harnesses. METR has published no time horizons for any of these models. Fable 5.2 and Hydrogen have no Arena rating yet.
- Open conflicts: whether Carbon went to all paying API customers on 10 Nov (Antigravity still says Ultra only); Hydrogen's output limit (65K per segment vs 1M); and whether Codex Pro has a 5-hour window.
- On cross-family review, the only quantified study is Greptile's (a vendor, older Opus 4.7 vs GPT 5.5 models). Each family's reviewer scored about 2-3 points higher on P0/P1 bugs when reviewing the other family's pull requests. There were no significance tests and only two families. It supports reviewing across families but does not prove it. ([www.greptile.com](https://www.greptile.com/blog/model-inversion))
